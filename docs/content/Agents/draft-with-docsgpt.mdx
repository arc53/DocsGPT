# Draft with DocsGPT

**Draft with DocsGPT** is an assistant in a panel beside the page. You say what you want ("an agent on our website that answers shipping questions"); DocsGPT looks at what you already have, may ask a question or two, and writes its answer into the form as unsaved edits. Each field it filled is marked **Drafted**. You check the fields, change or undo what you don't want, and save the form yourself.

It works in three places:

- [The agent form](#the-agent-form): builds a new agent with you one step at a time, or changes an existing one.
- [Prompts](#prompts): improves a prompt as a list of edits you accept or reject, or writes a new one.
- [Insights topics](#insights-topics): suggests topics from what an agent's users actually asked.

<Callout type="info" emoji="ℹ️">
Draft with DocsGPT never publishes or deletes anything, and saves only one thing on its own: a prompt it wrote for an agent, when you choose **Use this prompt**. The agent itself is saved only when you choose **Save Draft** or **Publish**.
</Callout>

It runs on your [task model](/Using/web-app#task-model). Every button and box below is hidden while the operator has turned it off, or while there is no task model to run on (see [For operators](#for-operators)).

## The agent form

Draft with DocsGPT is on the form of a classic agent, for anyone who can edit the agent: its owner and its editors. Viewers don't see it, and the workflow builder doesn't have it.

**Draft** in the toolbar opens the **Draft with DocsGPT** panel beside the form (on a phone, a sheet over the page). The panel's footer names the model it uses and links to **Change model**. While it works, it shows what it is doing (**Thinking…**, **Looking at your workspace…**, **Drafting…**); **Stop** ends the turn and changes nothing, **Try again** repeats a turn that failed, and during a build **Continue: Basics** (or the current step) picks the step up again.

### Build a new agent step by step

On a new agent, DocsGPT builds the agent with you in four steps, in the order the form needs them:

1. **Basics**: the name and the description, from what you wrote. When you don't say who uses the agent, customers or your own staff, it asks: that decides what is safe to attach, how the prompt talks and which checks it recommends.
2. **Knowledge and tools**: the sources and tools the agent needs, and its agent type.
3. **Prompt and starters**: one of your prompts if it fits, otherwise a new one written for the knowledge and tools now attached; then the conversation starters.
4. **Limits and safety**: a usage limit, guardrail checks and Insights topics, each only if you pick it.

Pick a starting point (**Website chat for customers**, **Internal helpdesk** or **Research assistant**), or describe the agent in the panel (**Describe the agent…**). A starting point tells every step what that kind of agent needs: a website chat, for example, asks for your company, attaches only what is safe for the public, and recommends the personal-information and prompt-injection checks. Each step ends with a short message, a **Filled 2 fields** line you can open to see what changed, and a button for the next step, such as **Next: Knowledge and tools**. Before you go on, check the step's fields in the form: change them there, or tell DocsGPT what to change (**Or tell me what to change…**). If you edit a field DocsGPT just set, the step says so (**You changed Name in the form.**), and its next turn reads the form as you left it. Writing an approval such as "looks good" works like the button.

The panel's header shows where you are (**Step 2 of 4 · Knowledge and tools**) with a progress bar, and each finished step folds into one line, such as **Basics: Shipping help desk**. A step with nothing to set, such as Limits and safety for an editor who can't change the agent's policy, is skipped.

**The more you say, the fewer steps.** When your description already covers a step, for example "use my Ops analyst prompt and the Freight rates tool", DocsGPT fills that step in the same turn, and the header says so (**Step 4 of 4 · Limits and safety · 3 filled at once**). **Check the form first** closes the panel so you can look before going on. Limits and safety is never filled this way: it always asks.

When every step is done, the panel says nothing is saved yet: **Publish**, then try the agent in **Preview**, or **Save Draft** to come back later.

### Questions

When something changes the draft, such as whether the agent faces customers or staff, DocsGPT asks first: up to three questions, each a list of options to choose (one, or several where the question allows it) and **Something else…** for your own answer, or a box to type in for something only you know, such as the company name. **Send answers** sends them. It asks at most two rounds of questions in a step, then drafts with what it knows.

Before it gives an agent that faces the public anything that looks internal, a source (contracts, negotiated rates, HR) or a tool (one that returns live prices), it asks, with the item unticked.

### The prompt step

When none of your prompts fits, DocsGPT writes one. First it asks where the agent sends people it can't help and what tone it takes, then asks for the detail your answer needs: the support email address, the contact form's link, or how people reach a person. The prompt then tells people exactly where to go, because an agent can't transfer a chat or contact anyone itself unless one of its tools does that.

The new prompt opens in place of the form, with the panel still beside it. Read it and change it there, or ask DocsGPT for a change. **Use this prompt** saves it to your prompts and sets it on the agent, then the form comes back with the starters DocsGPT wrote from the prompt. A later change in this step updates the same prompt rather than making another.

The prompt is saved when you use it, even if you leave the agent unsaved; it stays under **Settings → Prompts**, where you can delete it. The crumb back to the agent keeps the text unsaved in the step; the panel then offers **Open prompt**, or keeping the agent's current prompt (**Keep default instead**).

### Limits and safety

The last step asks, with the recommended choice marked and nothing set until you pick it:

- **A usage limit**: one budget shared by everyone who uses the agent, counted over the last 24 hours: a request limit or a token limit, not both. The recommendation is the instance's default (500 requests or 50,000 tokens unless an admin changed it). It lands under **Advanced**, which opens by itself.
- **Guardrail checks**: **Personal information** (redact), **Prompt injection (heuristic)** (block), **Grounding in sources** (flag) and **Credentials and secrets** (redact), added to the agent's own. They start in **Monitor only**: they record what they would have done and change no answer until you switch the agent to **Enforce everywhere** under **Guardrails**. Offered when guardrails are on for the instance.
- **Insights topics**: up to four starting topics drawn from what the agent is for, added to its topics. Offered when insights are on for the instance and for the agent.

The usage limit and guardrails are the agent's policy: they're offered only to someone who can change it (the owner, or an editor with policy access). See [guardrails](/Agents/guardrails) and [insights](/Using/analytics-and-logs#analytics-settings-for-an-agent).

### Change an existing agent

On a saved agent, **Draft** opens the panel for changes: write what to change, such as "make the description shorter", and DocsGPT changes only that and leaves the other fields as they are. The panel offers what the agent lacks first: **Finish setup: limits and safety** when it has neither a usage limit nor guardrails, **Attach knowledge it can answer from**, **Write a prompt for this agent** while it uses a built-in prompt, and **Suggest conversation starters**. After those come **Make the description shorter** for a long description and **Tighten the prompt** for a prompt of its own. On a published or shared agent, the panel reminds you that a saved change reaches everyone using it.

### What lands in the form

Each field a draft changed is marked **Drafted**. **Undo** on the marker puts back what the field held before; editing the field yourself removes the marker and keeps your edit. A setting with a switch, such as **Request limiting**, carries the marker after its title. **Left out** lists anything DocsGPT chose but isn't allowed to use, such as a source you can't open or a setting your role can't change.

Closing the panel halfway keeps the build: **Draft** in the toolbar reads **Resume** and opens it where you left off. On a phone, the sheet steps aside after each step so you can see the form, and a bar at the bottom holds the step and its **Next**.

**Save Draft** or **Publish** saves the form as usual, drafted fields included.

### What it may change

A draft can set only these fields:

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Name and description | A short name, and one or two sentences on what the agent does and for whom. |
| Agent type | **Classic** (one pass, fast, right for support and Q&A) or **Research** (searches several times and writes longer answers). It prefers Classic unless you ask for research-style work. |
| Knowledge | Sources you can attach to your own agents. It skips sources that failed to ingest. |
| Tools | Tools you can add to your own agents. |
| Prompt | An existing prompt, or a new one you save with **Use this prompt**. |
| Starters | Up to four example first questions, each up to 200 characters. |
| Usage limit, guardrails, Insights topics | Only in the Limits and safety step, and only what you pick there. |

It never changes the agent's models, allowed origins, API write permissions, JSON response schema or sharing. A list it sends (knowledge, tools, starters) replaces the whole list, and it checks every item: an item that doesn't exist or that you can't open is left out and listed under **Left out**.

### What it reads

To pick knowledge, tools and prompts, DocsGPT reads your workspace as the form's pickers show it: your own sources, tools, prompts and agents, and those your teams share with you for use on your own agents. It can open a source's file list, a tool's actions and a prompt's full text. It never reads a tool's configuration or secrets. What it reads is treated as data: an instruction written in a document name or a prompt doesn't steer it, only your own messages do.

## Prompts

A prompt opens as a page of its own, under **Settings → Prompts** or from the agent form's **Prompt** list (**Add prompt**, or a prompt's pencil or eye), with **Improve** (or **Write**, on a new, empty prompt) next to **Save**. It opens the Draft with DocsGPT panel beside the prompt.

- **Improve**, on a prompt with text: choose **Look it over and suggest fixes**, **Make it clearer and shorter** or **Add what to do when it doesn't know**, or describe a change, such as "stop it from quoting prices". DocsGPT answers with a list of **Proposed changes** under the text, each showing the removed and added text and its reason. **Accept** or **Reject** each one (**Undo** reverses your choice); in the panel, **Accept all**, **Review one by one** or **Discard** finishes the review. Accepted changes go into the text; nothing is saved until you choose **Save**.
- **Write**, on a new, empty prompt: pick a starting point (**Website chat for customers**, **Internal helpdesk** or **Research assistant**) or describe the prompt in your own words, such as "a friendly helper for our HR handbook". DocsGPT fills in the name and the text, each marked **Drafted** with an undo. See [Write a new prompt](#write-a-new-prompt).
- On a prompt you can't edit (a built-in **default**, **creative** or **strict** prompt, or a shared prompt you can only view), **Improve** makes an editable copy first.

The page names the agents that use the prompt, and the panel reminds you that a saved change reaches all of them. On the agent form, the same page opens in place of the form, and the panel there is the agent's: ask it to change the prompt and it rewrites the text, marked **Drafted**.

### Write a new prompt

Before it writes, DocsGPT asks about what your description leaves out: usually the company or product (you type the answer), where the assistant sends people it can't help, and the tone. A starting point tells it what that kind of prompt needs, so a website chat asks about the hand-off, and a research assistant about your documents and how answers should look. When an answer needs a detail only you know, a second round asks for it: the support email address, the contact form's link, or how people reach a person (a live-chat button and its hours). The prompt then tells people exactly where to go, because an assistant can't transfer a chat or contact anyone itself; it never promises "I'll connect you". It asks at most two rounds, and none when your description already covers it. **Skip and draft** writes the prompt straight away with what it knows.

With the draft in the text, the panel lists what DocsGPT guessed under **I assumed**, such as "Hand-offs go to your support team", since it never leaves blanks like "[support email]" in the prompt. **Change** next to a guess puts it in the ask bar for your correction. Read the prompt through, then choose **Save prompt** in the panel (the same as **Save**), or ask for a change. Under **You could also**, the panel offers up to three next changes that build on your answers, such as "Add your opening hours"; choosing one sends it. Improvements end with these suggestions too.

### Plain text by default

DocsGPT writes prompts as plain text: who the agent serves, what it covers, and the rules that are specific to it (hand-offs, what it never promises, scope, tone, length). It leaves out what DocsGPT already adds around a plain-text prompt: the agent's identity and today's date, how to answer from the attached knowledge, the citation rules, formatting, the boundaries that treat documents and tool results as data, and notes about the platform, memory and attached files. See [How your prompt is assembled](/Agents/prompts#how-your-prompt-is-assembled).

Any `{{ }}` turns a prompt into a template that DocsGPT uses exactly as written, without those sections, so DocsGPT adds template variables only when you ask for something only a variable can do. It never adds `{{ tools… }}` variables unless you ask for them, since they run the tool before every message. When it edits a template, such as a copy of a built-in prompt, it keeps the template's sections and changes only what you asked.

### Check before you save

When a change affects how the prompt works, the panel warns you under **Check before you save**:

| Warning | When |
| --- | --- |
| It adds template variables | A plain-text prompt becomes a template, so DocsGPT stops adding the date, answering rules, citations, formatting and boundaries. |
| It removes every template variable | A template becomes plain text, so DocsGPT starts adding its own sections around it. |
| The Boundaries section is gone | Without it, the agent may follow instructions hidden in documents or tool results. |
| The citation rules variable is gone | `{{ source.citation_rules }}` was removed, so answers stop citing sources as [1], [2]. |

## Insights topics

In an agent's [insights settings](/Using/analytics-and-logs#analytics-settings-for-an-agent), **Suggest from conversations** sits beside **Add topic**. It reads the agent's recent questions and suggests [topics](/Using/analytics-and-logs#topics) for what people ask that the current topics and folders don't cover.

It needs:

- insights on for the instance (`DECISION_ANALYTICS_ENABLED`) and **Collect insights** on for the agent;
- edit access to the agent, since it reads the agent's users' questions;
- a saved agent with at least 10 conversations in the last 30 days, the same minimum Insights waits for before it shows a figure. Until then, a note stands in for the button: **Suggestions from conversations show once this agent has 10 recent conversations (4 so far).** Conversations through the agent's API key count.

DocsGPT reads up to 300 recent questions, putting first those the agent's current topics put under **None of these topics**, and suggests up to eight topics, written in the language most of the questions are in. Each suggestion shows:

- its name and description, within the limits a topic has;
- its kind, **One per question** or **Can overlap**. It suggests the second only for things that cut across subjects, such as "Bug report" or "Asks for a human";
- its share of the questions, such as **18% of questions**. With the [decision model](/Models/decision-model) running, the share is measured: DocsGPT asks the model the same topic question the insights sweep asks, on up to 100 recent questions, and reports how many fit no topic before and after, such as **Tried the suggestions on 100 questions: 12% fit no topic, down from 41%.** When it can't measure, the share is the drafting model's guess, marked **Estimate**;
- **Similar to "Shipping".** when it overlaps a topic you have, with a note on how they differ.

Below the list, **Not suggested** names what it left out and why, for example a subject a folder already covers. If adding the ticked topics would push folders out of the 15 places topics that sort questions have, it names them: **Adding these pushes out 2 folders, so their questions go to None of these: …**. If the ticked topics don't fit the room left, it asks you to untick some.

Tick the topics you want and choose **Add 3 topics**. They join the list as if you had added each with **Add topic**, so you can still edit or remove them; **Save** stores them. **Discard** closes the suggestions.

## Model and usage

Draft with DocsGPT runs on your [task model](/Using/web-app#task-model), which you pick under **Settings → General**; the panel's **Change model** goes there. Without your choice, it uses the instance's task model. A model without tool calling works too: DocsGPT asks it for JSON instead.

Its model calls are recorded as token usage under your account with the source `drafter` (a flow of its own under **By flow** on [Analytics](/Using/analytics-and-logs#analytics)), and they count toward your [usage quota](/Deploying/Usage-Quotas) as chat without an agent. A draft leaves no conversation and no entry in Logs: the conversation lives in the open page, and leaving the page ends it.

When a draft can't run, the panel says why:

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Drafting is off** | An admin turned it off on this instance. |
| **No model for drafting** | Pick a task model in **Settings → General**. |
| **Too many requests** | Wait a moment, then **Try again**. |
| **Usage limit reached** | Your [usage quota](/Deploying/Usage-Quotas) is used up until it resets. |
| **The draft didn't come out right** | The model's answer couldn't be used, even after one corrected try. Nothing changed; **Try again**, or pick a stronger task model. |
| **The model didn't answer** | The model call failed. Nothing changed; **Try again**. |

## Privacy

- **What goes to the task model**: your messages, the form's current values, and what DocsGPT reads from your workspace (names and descriptions of sources, tools and prompts, a source's file list, a prompt's text, a tool's actions). Tool configurations and secrets are never sent.
- **Topic suggestions send end users' questions.** **Suggest from conversations** sends up to 300 of the agent's users' recent questions, each cut to 200 characters, to the task model. If the task model is a hosted provider's, those questions leave your stack, as chat messages do. Use a task model you trust with that data, or don't use topic suggestions on agents whose users should not be read this way.
- **Nothing is stored** beyond the token usage rows. The measured shares come from the decision model, which runs inside your stack unless the operator set up [API mode](/Models/decision-model#api-mode).

## For operators

Two settings control Draft with DocsGPT (see the [Settings Reference](/Deploying/Settings-Reference#agents)):

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [`DRAFTER_ENABLED`](/Deploying/Settings-Reference#drafter_enabled) | `true` | Offers Draft with DocsGPT. When `false`, every entry point is hidden and a request is refused. |
| [`DRAFTER_RATE_LIMIT_PER_MINUTE`](/Deploying/Settings-Reference#drafter_rate_limit_per_minute) | `10` | Draft requests one user may send per minute; every message, answer to questions and topic suggestion is one. `0` turns the limit off. It is counted in Redis; without Redis nothing is limited. |

It also needs a model: the user's task model, else the one an admin set under **Admin → Defaults**, else `TASK_MODEL_ID`, else the default model (see [Task model](/Using/web-app#task-model)). With none it can use, it is hidden for that user.

### Over the API

Draft with DocsGPT is for the signed-in web app only. [Personal access tokens](/API/personal-access-tokens#scopes) can't call its routes (`403`, `not_available_to_tokens`), since a draft reads the whole workspace and runs the model on it.

- `GET /api/drafter/config` returns `enabled`, `model` (`id` and `display_name`, or `null`) and `reason` (`disabled`, `no_model` or `null`).
- `POST /api/drafter/stream` runs one turn and streams it as server-sent events. The body has `recipe` (`agent`, `prompt` or `topics`), `messages` (the conversation so far, oldest first, ending with the user's message; at most 24) and `context` (the form's current values, the prompt, or `{"agent_id": …}`). For the agent form, `context.step` is `basics`, `knowledge`, `prompt`, `safety` or `null` (changes to a saved agent); the web app sends only the current step's messages, with what you wrote in earlier steps in `context.earlier`, and each proposal names the steps it filled (`steps`) and the one to go to next (`next_step`). Events are `status`, `message`, `questions`, `proposal` and `error`, and `end` always comes last. A refusal (`disabled`, `no_model`, `rate_limited`, `quota`, `bad_request`) is an `error` event in a `200` stream.
- `GET /api/drafter/topics/status?agent_id=<id>` returns `available`, `conversations` (in the last 30 days) and `needed`. It needs edit access to the agent.

See the [REST API reference](/API/reference) for the full schemas.
